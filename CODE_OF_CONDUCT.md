# NAUX Code of Conduct

NAUX requires careful reasoning, reproducible evidence, and rigorous technical
review. Those standards are compatible with respectful treatment of people.
Critique the work and its claims; do not attack the person doing it.

This policy governs conduct. [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) governs
contribution value, architectural fit, and technical acceptance. Neither policy
is a substitute for the other.

## Scope and Equal Treatment

This policy applies to everyone participating in NAUX project spaces, including
contributors, reporters, reviewers, maintainers, and the project owner.

Project spaces include this repository's issues, pull requests, reviews,
comments, discussions, and other explicitly designated NAUX community channels.
It also applies when someone represents the project in an official capacity,
and to project-related harassment carried into private messages or other
channels. It does not claim authority over unrelated personal activity.

Participation must be free from harassment and discrimination based on age,
disability, ethnicity, nationality, race, religion, gender identity or
expression, sexual orientation, socioeconomic background, education, experience,
or language proficiency. Technical experience, project ownership, and past
contributions do not exempt anyone from this policy.

## Expected Conduct

- Address the specific claim, implementation, evidence, or behavior under
  discussion. Explain disagreements with reasons and relevant references.
- Be direct without insults, humiliation, or assumptions about another
  person's intelligence, motives, or worth.
- Recognize that unfamiliarity with NAUX, imperfect English, honest mistakes,
  and requests for clarification are not misconduct.
- Accept corrections, acknowledge errors, and distinguish what is known from
  what remains uncertain. Apply this equally to code and review comments.
- Respect privacy, attribution, personal boundaries, and requests to stop
  unwanted contact.
- Keep discussions relevant and allow others to disengage. Follow reasonable
  moderation directions without escalating into personal conflict.

## Technical Disagreement Is Not Misconduct

Maintainers may request stronger evidence, question a design, require narrower
scope, or reject work that fails NAUX's acceptance criteria. Contributors may
challenge an implementation, contract, benchmark, review, or maintainer decision
in good faith. Neither disagreement nor a declined contribution is, by itself,
a conduct violation.

A duplicate issue, missing test, incorrect result, or unsuccessful proposal
normally calls for technical feedback or issue triage—not a conduct sanction.
Repeated review rounds must remain about the work, not pressure to accept
personal abuse. A request for respectful treatment does not require merging a
PR; a rigorous review does not justify harassment.

## Unacceptable Conduct

- Threats, intimidation, stalking, targeted harassment, discriminatory abuse,
  or encouraging others to engage in those behaviors.
- Sexual harassment, unwanted sexual attention, or sexualized content directed
  at participants.
- Personal attacks, demeaning language, or deliberate public humiliation.
- Publishing or threatening to publish private information, correspondence,
  or identifying details without permission.
- Impersonation, deliberate misattribution, or knowingly fabricated evidence.
  Honest errors, disputed interpretations, and inconclusive results are not
  equivalent to intentional deception.
- Sustained disruption, coordinated pile-ons, spam, repeated unwanted contact,
  or evading moderation restrictions through other accounts or channels.
- Retaliation against someone for making a good-faith report, participating in
  its review, or raising a conduct concern.

Good-faith reports remain protected even when the available information does
not establish a violation. Knowingly false accusations or reports used to
harass someone are themselves misconduct.

## Reporting a Concern

Do not post sensitive incident details, private messages, or personal data in a
public issue. You do not need to confront the person involved before reporting.

A dedicated private NAUX conduct email address has not yet been published.
Until one is confirmed, the available routes for incidents on GitHub are:

- Use **Report content** on the relevant issue, PR, or comment. If GitHub offers
  **Report to repository admins**, it can notify the repository maintainers.
  Availability depends on GitHub permissions and repository settings.
- For suspected violations of GitHub's rules, use **Report abuse to GitHub
  Support** or the contact form linked in [GitHub's reporting
  instructions](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).
  This is also an external route when reporting to repository maintainers is
  unavailable or inappropriate, including concerns involving the owner.

GitHub reviews reports under its own policies; it is not NAUX's appeal board,
and reporting to GitHub does not necessarily notify NAUX maintainers. Do not
assume this provides a private project reporting channel for off-platform
incidents. If you choose to request a private contact through a public project
thread, include only the request—not names, allegations, or incident details.
You are not required to make that public request.

When a suitable private channel is available, provide what you can safely
share: relevant links or messages, dates, a description of the conduct, any
ongoing safety concern, and a safe way to contact you. A complete investigation,
formal proof, or public disclosure is not a prerequisite for raising a concern.

Maintainers should restrict access to reports to those needed to handle them,
avoid unnecessary identifying details, and explain any necessary sharing where
feasible. Absolute confidentiality or anonymity cannot be promised. This is a
volunteer project, not an emergency service; no response-time guarantee is
offered.

Software vulnerabilities follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), not this reporting
process. Ordinary technical support follows [SUPPORT.md](SUPPORT.md).

## Review and Enforcement

Maintainers responsible for moderation should consider the available context,
severity, impact, and any pattern of conduct. Apply the same standard regardless
of someone's role or contribution history. Where safe and appropriate, allow
the people involved to clarify the facts without requiring direct confrontation
or exposing the reporter's identity.

Actions should be proportionate and may include:

1. A request to correct behavior or stop a particular interaction.
2. A warning identifying the conduct and the change required.
3. Removal or hiding of harmful content, locking a thread, or a temporary
   restriction on participation.
4. A permanent participation ban for severe or repeated misconduct.

These are possible responses, not a mandatory sequence. Credible threats,
doxxing, or other serious harm may require immediate protective restrictions.
Where feasible, explain the reason, duration, and conditions of a restriction
privately, without publishing sensitive report material. Distinguish a
temporary protective action from a final finding.

## Conflicts of Interest and Reconsideration

A maintainer directly involved in a complaint must not be its sole reviewer.
Use an uninvolved maintainer when one is available. If there is no independent
project reviewer, disclose that limitation rather than presenting the decision
as an independent investigation. GitHub's reporting route remains available
for matters within GitHub's policies; NAUX does not claim to have an external
enforcement team.

Reporters and affected participants may request reconsideration through the
same safe reporting route, identifying new information or a specific procedural
concern. Use an uninvolved reviewer where available. Do not reopen the dispute
by contacting the reporter, evading restrictions, or organizing public pressure.
Respectful requests for reconsideration must not result in retaliation.
